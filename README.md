# cp0x DeFi token value research

Repository `cp0x-defi-token-value-research` by cp0x (https://cp0x.com). Published 2026-10-08, version 1.0. This repository holds the data, code, frozen methods, change logs and independent reviews for every study in the series. Pages in English, Russian and Chinese are on https://cp0x.com/blog.

## What can a DAO do for the value of its token?

This synthesis by cp0x summarises six studies of DAO (decentralised autonomous organisation) decisions, protocol businesses and their tokens from 2022 to 2026, with every token return measured against ETH (Ether). Across the series we found no robust evidence that buybacks, direct distributions or burns raise a token's price by themselves, and no evidence that a more direct form beats a buyback. In study 3 (202 observations of 110 protocols, October 2023 to October 2026), fee growth was the only pre-registered factor associated with token returns against ETH that survived all pre-specified checks in the main 2023–2026 analysis, and 10% more fee growth went with about 2.5% more token return against ETH over the same year. In study 4, protocols that kept a larger share of their fees were not valued higher at equal fee scale, and cheap tokens did not later outperform expensive ones. In study 6, 54 of 58 major spending and incentive decisions at Lido, Aave, Sky and Uniswap stated a goal, but only 14 stated it in terms public data can check. The main estimates come from the 2023–2026 regime; the supplementary 2022–2023 window is small. All results describe associations, not proven causes.

The full synthesis page is at https://cp0x.com/blog/what-can-a-dao-do-for-its-token-value

## Key findings

- In the cp0x series, study 1 (43 events in 16 DeFi protocols, 2022 to 2026) found a positive reaction on both controls only for the first public announcement of a buyback programme, with a 30-day median of 4.2 percentage points above a basket of large DeFi tokens and 12.0 points above similar tokens, based on just seven events with a spread of about 40 points.
- In cp0x study 2 (seven cases of introducing or switching the form of value transfer, 2022 to 2025), no form had a consistent advantage, six of the seven came in below the main control, and the study found nothing in favour of payouts in ETH or stablecoins over buybacks.
- In cp0x study 3 (202 observations of 110 DeFi protocols, October 2023 to October 2026), fee growth was the only pre-registered factor that survived all pre-specified checks in the main 2023–2026 analysis, and a protocol whose fees grew 10% faster than a peer's saw its token beat the peer by about 2.5% against ETH on average over the same year.
- In cp0x study 4 (DeFi protocols in October-to-October periods 2023 to 2026), protocols that kept a larger share of fees were not valued higher at equal fee scale, and a protocol with twice the fees was worth only about 30% more in market capitalisation on average.
- In cp0x study 5 (Lido, Aave, Sky and Uniswap, 2023 to 2025), neither of two automatic methods recovered reported DAO spending from treasury outflows, and the ratio of transfer outflow to reported spending ranged from 0.15 at Sky to 6.4 at Uniswap.
- In cp0x study 6 (112 DAO decisions at Lido, Aave, Sky and Uniswap executed 2023 to 2025), 54 of 58 major spending and incentive decisions stated a goal, but only 14 stated it in terms public data can check, and no pattern among those 14 was robust across DAOs and counting choices.
- In a supplementary cp0x window from October 2022 to October 2023 (28 observations), the study 3 fee growth coefficient was 0.21 against 0.26 in the main periods, with the same sign but significance that was sensitive to the choice of standard errors and to individual tokens.

Each study folder has the same layout. `README.md` holds the summary, file list, data dictionary and reproduction steps. `data/` holds curated CSV and results JSON files. `method/` holds the frozen method files with their sha256 and freeze times, change logs and independent review reports. `code/` holds the scripts and a `code/README.md` with the run order. `MANIFEST.sha256` lists the sha256 of every file in the folder.

## How the folders relate

The studies run in sequence, and each one addresses a weakness of the one before.

- Study 1 (folder 01) dates buyback, distribution and supply-reduction decisions and measures the price reaction by stage. Study 2 (folder 02) asks whether the form of value transfer matters, and it uses a 55-event database of day-dated events (`data/events.csv`) to exclude control tokens with events of their own.
- Study 3 (folder 03) moves from events to annual trajectories of 110 protocols and decomposes each token's return against ETH into fee growth, rerating and dilution. Study 4 (folder 04) takes the study 3 panels (`panel.csv`, `panel_rep.csv`) and helper code as inputs and splits returns further into category growth, market share, monetisation and rerating.
- Folder 07 reruns the main tests of studies 3 and 4 on one earlier window with the same code. Its scripts must be run from the study 3 and study 4 working directories, so it depends on folders 03 and 04.
- Study 5 (folder 05) tests whether DAO spending can be measured from treasury outflows. Because both methods failed, study 6 (folder 06) became case studies of the four DAOs whose actual spending is reported (Lido, Aave, Sky and Uniswap).
- This folder (00) summarises all of them. The internal names used inside the folders map to the public numbering as follows. Study 1 is Research 2, study 2 is Research 3, study 3 is Research 4, study 4 is Research 5, study 5 is Research 6 validation, study 6 is Research 6, and the supplementary window is Window 2022.
